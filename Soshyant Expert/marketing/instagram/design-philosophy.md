# Signal Cartography

Signal Cartography treats knowledge as a faint signal moving through darkness. Each composition is an instrument reading, a survey plate from some discipline that measures meaning. The ground is near-total black, a void with faint structure: a dot lattice at a fixed pitch and scanlines so fine they show only up close. Against it, light is rationed. A single neon jade carries truth and permission. Hot pink marks loss, exposure and alarm. Amber is the query, the one moving thing in the field. No other colours are allowed, and every use of one is a decision weighed with painstaking care.

Space is organised like a heads-up display: corner brackets instead of borders, measured margins and a fixed rhythm of top rail, field and baseline. Forms are geometric and exact: squares with clipped corners, dashed perimeters, hairline vectors joining points. Dense accumulations of marks, such as clusters of hundreds of points or rows of identical cells, reward a second look. Within that accumulation, one or two things are lit and everything else stays quiet. The eye goes to the lit thing first, and that is where the message sits.

Scale is the main tool. One enormous figure, a number or a condensed headline set very large, sits against tiny monospaced annotations that read like the labels on a technical plate. There are no middle sizes, and the tension between the monumental and the minute does the explaining. Type is part of the structure: condensed display letters stacked like girders, mono labels lined up to the grid, small reference codes (`§7.2`, `01 / 05`, `[ OK ]`) placed as a cartographer places coordinates.

Composition follows a sequence. Each plate is self-contained, and together they read like a survey: signal found, loss measured, mechanism drawn, perimeter sealed, channel opened. Each plate repeats the frame and changes the subject inside it, so the set feels like one document studied over time. Balance comes from alignment and restraint rather than symmetry. Nothing touches an edge or collides, and every element keeps the same breathing room from its neighbours.

The finish must read as master-level work: something meticulously crafted over countless hours by someone at the top of their field. Hairlines are hairlines, glows are soft rather than gaudy, and spacing is measured, not eyeballed. Text appears only where the drawing cannot speak for itself: a few words, set with precision.
